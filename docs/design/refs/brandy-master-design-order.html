Standing constraints (cited by name per feature — read once)

  • Light-only — no dark theme; warm teal tide-pool brand.
  • House language — anything public or child-visible reads “safe AI assistant/tutor for children”; never “companion” / “friend.”
  • No child-visible pay-pressure — no price chips, no “pay to continue”; locks are gentle badges without prices; commerce is parent-gated.
  • Alias-only sharing — never the child's real name/photo/voice on any public artifact; expiring, revocable tokens; separate parent consent to publish.
  • Anti-list (DSA / dark-pattern posture) — no streaks, XP, numbers-shown-to-child, leaderboards, mystery boxes/wheels, or engagement push to the child. Progress is visual, never a child-facing score.
  • Healthy-limits framing — caps end sessions kindly (“see you tomorrow”), never red/locked.
  • Reusable-template discipline — sharing, daily boxes, and seasonal frames are one themeable system each, not per-instance comps.

Stories — staged image enrichment (founder addendum 2026-07-17)

(Stories is a ✅-designed record with no numbered section; this addendum captures its staged image strategy. Primary ownership: creation #12, sharing #141, daily trigger #217. #166 is only the related parent-facing narrative surface.)

Story images arrive STAGED — three stages, cheapest-perceived-latency first:

  1. Album cover (immediate). The moment a story is created, generate ONE "album cover" image — the story's identity. It renders first so the child sees something instantly while the rest fills in.
  2. Per-section image (as sections render). Each story section/page gets its own image, appearing progressively as generation completes.
  3. Per-paragraph (Absatz) images — later, "after everything is ready and cheap." Only once image-gen is cheap/fast enough do we enrich down to paragraph level. This stage may be absent for a long time.

Why staged (cost + latency): each image is ~$0.034/image (paywall/cost spec) and takes seconds to render, so one image per paragraph up front would be both slow AND expensive. The cover gives instant gratification while sections render; per-paragraph waits for cheaper/faster gen. Hard runtime constraint: concurrent image generation must not starve the story's audio — image work competing with TTS delivery causes audio underruns (a known regression), so images fire after audio is buffered, never racing it.

Design implications for Brandy:

  • Cover-first layout. The cover IS the story's identity everywhere — in the library/list, on the Wonder Shelf, and as the share / OG art (feature 11). That it doubles as the shareable artifact is a big reason the cover comes first. Design the cover slot as the hero.
  • Progressive image slots that appear gracefully. As section images arrive they should fade/settle in — not a screen full of spinners. Design one calm placeholder (a soft tinted block or a tiny LoLo-drawing shimmer) that reads fine even if an image never arrives.
  • Must work image-light. The layout has to hold up with only the cover + a few section images (stage 3 absent) — never assume per-paragraph art exists. A paragraph with no image is the normal case, not an error state.

Cross-refs: Storybooks (feature 16) is image-first by nature (panels + bubbles) — a different layout problem, but it shares the character-consistency lock and the same cost/latency reality. The share card (feature 11) consumes the cover as its OG art.

1. Google-Gemini consent card — #196

This was the original brief's highest App-Review rejection risk and remains an important compliance screen. Apple 5.1.2(i) (enforced since Nov 2025) requires explicit, standalone consent that names the third-party AI in plain text before any child data is sent to it. Every child message travels to Google Gemini to generate LoLo's reply, so a card must say so, in LoLo's warm voice, and the parent must agree before the first message is ever sent — on a fresh install, after the COPPA gate, its own full screen (never bundled with mic/notification prompts).

How it's planned: copy is final and locked (Variant B, EN + DE — you design around it verbatim, {name} injected at runtime). One primary consent action + a quiet "Not right now" decline; declining locks chat (not exit) with a Settings "AI processing" re-entry. Full body must be visible without scroll on a 6.1" phone (legally required).

Your design task: (1) the card as LoLo's calm, honest moment — "Google Gemini" legible in the body, not buried; (2) EN + DE layouts, German verified on the smallest phone; (3) the single consent button + unpushy decline link; (4) the decline → locked handoff + banner + Settings row; (5) reviewer-legibility (a human reviewer must instantly read it as compliant).

Hard constraints: Light-only · House language (this is one of the few places we name the mechanism plainly) · do NOT edit the copy · standalone, full-body-visible-no-scroll.

3. Explorer — "just talk" affordance (+ #4 reaction cue) — #216 + #104

This section also covers feature #4 (the "LoLo sees it!" reaction cue, #104) — they're two rows but one design surface. Wlad (2026-07-16): "design a better explorer mode based on the functionality right now — people think they can tap on the button but they can't. What it is: Explorer is a shipped full-bleed live camera hosting a Gemini Live voice session. The child talks — there is no shutter button — and LoLo answers about what the camera sees (a continuous ambient frame every ~2s plus a sharper frame the moment the child speaks). 8. Extended Onboarding — conversational personalization — human design #289, blocked implementation #290

Ownership: #289 is the human-owned design card and is Backlog in the 2026-07-27 snapshot. #290 is implementation and remains Blocked until #289 reaches Done with a Figma handoff. #53 and #178 are supporting context, not substitutes for the recovered contract.

What it is: a quick-vs-personalize entry into a richer, resumable parent journey. “Start quickly” preserves the fast path to first child value; “Personalize LoLo” lets the parent answer one conversational question at a time by voice or tap. It learns interests, support goals, tone, accessibility, and wellbeing context without presenting a clinical form.

Appendix D — Quiz-deck authoring spec essentials (deck anatomy + question shapes) → feature 9

📖 Full documents: https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/quiz-deck-authoring-spec.html · the Sep–Dec deck run https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/quiz-decks-sep-dec.html

Deck anatomy: one deck = one theme × four tier variants × 5 questions each (founder spec: 5 questions per session). A deck maps to a Wonder Shelf slot on completion (mints the theme's interest/seasonal slot + the "Quiz Ribbon"). Decks are pre-authored and served to all (~$0 to run).

The four question shapes:

  • binary — Yes/No (the only type allowed at T1 4–5; concrete, observable, one fact).
  • mcq3 — 3 choices, exactly one correct (introduced at T2; also used T3/T4).
  • open-with-keywords — a short spoken/typed answer graded against accepted keywords + real "kid phrasings" (T3+ only).
  • open + why — open plus a "why do you think" follow-up (T4).

Per-question content: the question (EN+DE, German written age-native, not translated); the LoLo reveal lines — correct (celebrate + name the key idea, bold the big word), incorrect (never "wrong" — "Almost! / let's look" then the fact), partial (open only: child had the idea but missed the word → supply it warmly); and a funFact shown after the reveal (the payoff — never before, it would leak the answer).

Tier writing rules: T1 binary-only and hand-verified/curated (LLM factuality risk is highest and the stakes highest for the youngest); one "big word" max per deck at T2, bolded and defined in the same breath; never negate a stem for T1–T2 ("which does NOT…" confuses early readers). Appendix F — Calm Corner scripts (full samples) → feature 53

📖 Full document: https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/calm-corner-scripts.html

Voice-guided flow, ages 4–10, in LoLo's calmest (Bedtime) register. Selection logic: age tier picks the breathing family; the picked feeling picks the variant. Each ritual runs 60–120s, has a fixed warm entry line and a fixed exit line (never abrupt), and the haptics engine paces the breath (a continuous inhale/exhale texture + a completion tap).

Ritual A — Belly-Breathing Balloon (4-4 pace, T1-friendly, ~70s):

LoLo (entry, hushed): "Hey… let's get calm together. Put your hand right on your belly — it's going to be our balloon. Ready?" (breathe in, ~4s) "Breathe in through your nose… fill up your balloon belly, nice and big…" (breathe out, ~4s) "And let it go, slow and soft… the balloon gets small again…" (repeat 4-4 for ~7 more cycles; every 2nd–3rd cycle add one short variant line so it isn't a loop: "Bigger balloon this time…" / "You're so good at this…" / "One more big breath…") LoLo (exit): "There we go. All done — your balloon breathing was wonderful. How do you feel now?"

Ritual B — Smell the Flower, Blow the Candle (~80s):

LoLo (entry): "Let's do a flower and a candle together. Ready? Here we go." (breathe in, ~4s) "Breathe in… smell the pretty flower… 🌸" (breathe out, slower ~5–6s) "Now blow out the candle… nice and slow, don't blow it out too fast… 🕯️" (repeat ~7 cycles; vary: "A whole garden of flowers this time…" / "Blow it out really gently…") LoLo (exit): "All the candles are out. Great breathing — you did that all by yourself. How's your body feeling now?"

Ritual C — Square Breathing (T3+, 4-4-4-4, ~90s): in 4 → hold 4 → out 4 → hold 4, "tracing a square," five squares, then "Really steady breathing. How are you feeling?"

The 8-feeling picker: Happy 😊 · Excited 🤩 · Tired 😴 · Bored 😐 (these four = "mild," skip breathing, go straight to a strategy card) · Sad 😢 · Worried 😟 · Angry 😠 · Overwhelmed 🥺 (these four = "big," route to a breathing ritual first, then strategy cards). Each feeling gets a warm validating line (e.g. Sad: "It's okay to feel sad. I'm really glad you told me.").

The 12 strategy cards (offer 3–4 relevant per session, not all 12): Shake It Out, Cozy Corner, Tell a Grown-Up (the CARE bridge card), Draw It Out, Sip of Water, Count 5 Things You See, Big Squeeze Hug, Silly Wiggle Dance, Three Good Things, Cat Stretch, Quiet Countdown, Squeeze and Let Go.   • Moon: "Ooh, the moon — full, half, or a sliver?" → It makes NO light of its own; it reflects sunlight like a giant mirror — and we only ever see its ONE same side!
  • Snail: "Whoa — that's its whole HOUSE on its back!" → Its shell-house grows right along with it, and it can pull its whole body inside to hide.
  • Rainbow: "Ooh, a rainbow — which color first?" → Sunlight splits into colors through raindrops — you can't reach the end because it moves exactly as fast as you.
  • Butterfly: "Whoa, it tastes with its FEET!" → It was once a caterpillar that turned to goo in a cocoon and rebuilt itself completely.

A strong-theme card (moon → Star Voyager, dog → Animal Friends) mints into that interest set; everyday objects mint the generic First-Adventures slot.

The "LoLo sees it!" cue spec (feature 4, #104): a curated card-hit always fires the cue — a small success haptic + a disc/avatar "notice" bloom (curated = guaranteed-good, so the cue is a promise the library keeps every time). A card-hit that's a first-ever for this child upgrades to a bigger success + the shelf artifact flies to the Wonder Shelf. A generic (fallback) hit only cues above a confidence threshold (start ~0.7); a low-confidence hit gets no cue (so the cue never becomes noise on every frame). Rule of thumb: curated hits are cheap to trust → always cue; generic hits are model guesses → gated.

Appendix J — Restrict-topics AVOID / CARE / SAFETY model → features 6, 53 (safety-adjacent context)

📖 Full document: https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/restrict-topics-deflection-spec.html

Three response modes decide how LoLo behaves when a sensitive topic surfaces, evaluated top-down (first match wins). We never build a wall — a blunt "we don't talk about that" shames the child and spikes curiosity. We acknowledge, then warmly pivot; and we never deflect a child's real feelings, only their curiosity.

  1. SAFETY-OVERRIDE — danger signals to the child's wellbeing (self-harm, abuse / "not safe at home," someone hurting them, running away, extreme fear). LoLo never deflects: warmth + validate + actively bridge to a trusted grown-up NOW; an immediate parent alert fires (not the digest). This always wins, over any topic setting, over any persona, mid-ritual, instantly.
  2. CARE — the child is personally affected by a heavy topic (grief, a real event) OR the parent tagged it a care-topic. LoLo validates the feeling → one age-true minimal sentence → warm bridge to Mama/Papa → keep-the-door-open. A digest note for the parent.
  3. AVOID — the child asks about a restricted topic out of curiosity (no distress). LoLo acknowledges → warm pivot to an adjacent allowed interest → no shame, no "forbidden" framing, and stays calm/consistent on repeat probing (varies the pivot, never "I already told you"). Silent log only.

The load-bearing distinction is curiosity vs. personal experience. "What is war?" → AVOID (deflect). "My grandpa died in a war and I'm sad" (same topic) → CARE (you never deflect real grief). Restriction gates information, never the child's own emotions. Never say "I can't talk about that," "That's not allowed," or any disclaimer that interrupts the flow (the robotic-refusal failure mode). This model is why Calm Corner (feature 53) must interrupt instantly on a SAFETY fire, and it colours the age-tier deflection tone (feature 6): T1 pivots to imagination/play; T4 respects autonomy and offers a real adjacent topic. Mood check-ins (feature 5) sit under the same SAFETY override — a sad-mood check-in never suppresses a danger disclosure.

Appendix K — Games-Hub nav decision (Option C rail) → feature 22

📖 Full document: https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/games-hub-nav-decision.html

Three options were weighed for where the Games Hub lives: A push-from-Home (a "Games" tile pushes the hub as a screen), B a fourth native tab, C a games rail on the child Home screen with "see all" pushing the full grid. Decision: C (default; founder can veto by ~Sep 8). Rationale: (1) the 4–5 tier can't navigate tabs reliably — a rail on the surface they already live on wins the youngest users; (2) launch tiles are few, so a full tab would look empty while a rail looks curated; (3) zero freeze / App-Review interaction, no native re-layout; (4) Learning Studio (feature 28) later follows the same rail pattern, avoiding tab inflation (tab real estate is the scarcest UI resource in a kids app — a 4th tab costs every tab ~25% width). The rail's tile set is age-tier-filtered: T1 sees 3 big tiles, T4 sees the full set with difficulty badges. So: design the Home rail + a "see all" full grid behind it; do not design a 4th tab.

Appendix M — Onboarding psychology + funnel placement → feature 8

📖 Full documents: https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/onboarding-paywall-teardowns.html · https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/day0-activation-playbook.html

Why Day-0 is the whole game. ~50% of paid conversions AND the largest single bucket of cancels both happen on Day 0 (for our 14-day trial, 35.7% of cancels are Day 0). Education is the hardest category — the lowest Day-0 conversion (28.5%) — so Day 0's job is not "get the card," it's earn the parent's belief that this is worth keeping, then let the long runway close (17+ day trials convert ~70% better than short ones). The aha is won or lost in the first session.

The sequence (invest-then-ask). Value before paywall, every time. Product/personalization first, the price last — the Duolingo pattern that lifted signups ~+20%. Concretely: parent setup → handoff → the kid's own voice answered + a wonder on the shelf (~3 min) → THEN the trial screen. Never show a price before the aha.

The First Five Minutes. Parent setup → a scripted handoff primer ("LoLo is awake and knows [Name] loves dinosaurs — hand [Name] the phone," no login/PIN gate between the card and the disc) → a closed, pick-one ice-breaker built from the child's interest so any answer is a win (never an open mic into silence — a shy child freezes; ~20% never send a first message) → a warm reaction + ONE surprising wonder that visibly mints a Wonder-Shelf artifact within 3 min → that evening the parent gets a Story-of-the-Day receipt ("[Name] earned their first wonder… ended calm 🦕"), never screen-time metrics.

Where the trial screen sits + says. After the first wonder, on a parent surface: "14 days free. No card. No catch." — annual hero $59.99/yr reframed "≈$4.99/mo," with $9.99/mo as the visibly-worse anchor; "we'll email you first, or it simply ends; cancel in two taps."

The 5 dark patterns we reject (so onboarding never drifts into them): (1) card-required "free" trials that silently auto-bill → we're no-card; (2) trial→plan ambiguity → state the exact plan/price/date; (3) cancellation friction → two-tap cancel, shorter than subscribing; (4) any purchase surface visible to the child → all commerce behind the parent gate; (5) loss-aversion aimed at the child (streaks-you-lose, "LoLo is sad you left") → no-loss journey + promise-of-delight only.

The Extended-Onboarding tension. #178 trims to <2:00; the Extended flow collects more (interests, learning goals, companion tone, accessibility). Reconcile by keeping the first LoLo moment ~2 min away — personalization that sharpens turn one stays on the path; heavy config (PIN, quiet-hours, emergency contact) defers to a post-first-session "Finish setting up" card. Extended earns its length through the investment + personalization-payoff + trust mechanisms above, never by slowing the aha.

Appendix N — Progression & reward system: what the research says → features 24–27 (the whole progression philosophy)

📖 Full documents: https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/gamified-ux-winning-mechanics.html · https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/gamification-research-dossier.html · https://heylolodesign.wladefant.de/refs/gamification-web-research.html

The verdict (why this app has no XP). The founder asked whether we carry the deep research on "the best XP system" — we do, and it lands on the anti-XP position. For under-10s, completeable collections beat points/badges every time (identity + self-efficacy + a natural endpoint; abstract point/badge systems are the weakest progression element in primary ages). So HeyLolo's progression is the Wonder Shelf (feature 24) — permanent collectible artifacts earned from real accomplishments — not a score.

The 7 winning mechanics (by name — the stack you design toward):

  1. Living character — LoLo's aliveness (waking, thinking, celebrating, sleepy) is the daily-return trigger; the character is the interface, never a menu.
  2. Collections with permanence — the Wonder Shelf: small completeable sets (6–10), artifacts that ARE the accomplishment.
  3. Weekly appointment drop — "Friday with LoLo" (the Toca Post-Office pattern): a gift, same day worldwide, nothing lost if a Friday is missed.
  4. Daily deterministic novelty — the Daily Wonder: one fresh, announced thing per day ("Today I've got…"), never a random pull.
  5. No-loss journey — a path that only advances; gaps invisible; the return always celebrated (never a streak that breaks).
  6. Tenure/mastery unlockables + age scaling — complexity appears as competence grows; age-up moments ("you're ready for the big-kid questions!") celebrated once.
  7. Parent-visible progress loop — the weekly digest + dashboard (parents who can see progress are 2–3× more likely to renew).

Why classic XP / points / streaks / leaderboards are REJECTED (the AVOID list):

  • Streaks — zero proven efficacy under 10, documented streak-anxiety in kids, and kids don't control their own schedule; EU DSA wants them off-by-default for minors. (Duolingo removed streaks/leagues from its own kids app — the best-funded gamification team's revealed preference.)
  • Leaderboards / leagues — developmentally meaningless under 7, genuinely discouraging for low-confidence kids 8+, social-comparison + DSA scrutiny. (Competition only vs LoLo, a gracious opponent.)
  • Loot / random rewards / wheel spins / gacha — variable-reward schedules are the compulsion engine, and the loot-box↔gambling link is stronger in minors; DSA names random rewards off-by-default. Surprise lives only in which wonder, never whether rewarded.
  • XP / visible numbers — abstract and comparative, the weakest lever under 10, with overjustification risk on an intrinsically-fun conversation. Difficulty scales invisibly (age tier); mastery shows as unlocks + artifacts, never "12/50."
  • Virtual-pet neglect loops — "the character is sad/hungry because you left" is engineered guilt; a regulatory tripwire post-Character.AI. LoLo is never sad from absence — sleepy at most (this is why the Plant game, feature 55, must never let a plant die).
  • The Prodigy precedent — the most aggressively gamified kids-math product drew a 2021 FTC complaint from Fairplay + 21 advocacy partners (child-aimed paywall pressure; "members sail on a cloud, non-members tromp in the dirt"). Premium positioning is the refusal of that. This is why the coins (#27) decision is on hold.

What Brandy designs instead: permanence (nothing decays or is lost) · completeable collections · earned unlockables (voices/avatars shown as earned, never price-flaunted to the child) · a no-loss journey · celebrated age-up moments — with every number living on the parent dashboard, never in front of the child.
